I tried to write 1 (one) substack article

and this is what happened (spoiler: I failed)

A few months ago, I got it in my head that I would start a substack blog. All of the cool people have one (is what it seemed like to me). I would write about philosophy, life, culture, general musings. It would be a good way for me to sit down and work through my thoughts on a particular topic in a structured and regular way, allowing me to be more consious and intentional about various thoughts that I would typically just let float through my head, never becoming anything concrete.

With the typical overzealous excitement of starting any novel venture, I set up my substack blog (I need a suitably cute and whimsical name and picture obviously) and opened the substack post editor, cursor blinking in front of me. I need to write about something relevant, of course. Something that people will click on, and read, and that will leave a lasting impression. Something cultural but novel, something no one has thought to write about before but that anyone can relate to. I’ll write about physical media, CDs. I’ll write about whether the trend backwards towards CDs, LPs and other forms of physical media is in tension with the trend forwards towards sustainability and anti-consumerism, and where nostalgia, ownership and aesthetics play a part in that consideration. If you’ve been around on the internet (and substack in particular) at all you’ll know that physical media is a big topic of discussion, but I thought that my discussion could bring something new to the table, and that would be my big substack debut.
